AIDE MEMOIRE 
 
I. 
INTRODUCTION  
 
1. 
A World Bank team1 conducted a Mid-Term Review from October 29th – November 17th, 
2024, in Nairobi, Garowe, and virtually. The main objectives: (i) to assess project design and 
implementation progress towards achieving the Project Development Objectives; (ii) discuss 
detailed work plans, institutional arrangements, progress on the results framework, and 
disbursement targets; (iii) review fiduciary and environmental and social framework (ESF) 
compliance; (iv) agree on course corrections and next steps for improving project performance; and 
(v) discussion with Hargeisa city. Additionally, conduct technical discussions on the ongoing 
preparatory diagnostics for the proposed Somalia Informal Settlements Upgrading Project. 
 
2. 
The team would like to thank the Minister, State Minister, and Director General (DG) of 
the federal Ministry of Public Works, Reconstruction and Housing (MPWRH), Federal State 
Minister MPWRH, Mayors from participating cities, Project Coordination Unit (PCU), Project 
Implementation Units (PIUs) of participating cities, United Nations Office for Project Services 
(UNOPS), and International Organization for Migration (IOM), representatives from the European 
Union (EU), Germany Kreditanstalt für Wiederaufbau (KfW), and Foreign, Commonwealth, and 
Development Office, UK (FCDO). This Aide Memoire summarizes the key findings, 
recommendations, and agreements reached during the MTR. Table 1 shows key project data. As 
agreed during the meetings with the Government, this Aide Memoire is classified as an official 
document as required by the World Bank Access to Information Policy.

II. 
KEY AREAS FOR ATTENTION 
 
3. 
Accelerating project implementation. With current project closing date of December 31, 
2026, the mission identified actions needed for expediting physical progress, particularly in 
Dhusamereb, Beledweyne, and Mogadishu. For Mogadishu, roads contracts will be awarded by 
December 2024. Beledweyne and Dhusamareb have initiated procurement process and will award 
contract for their first construction packages by February 2025. Key actions include monthly check-
ins with each city and UNOPS and updating the Management Information System (MIS) on 
progress of activities in each city. Third party monitoring will commence in June 2025 for semi-
annual continuous activity moving forward.  
 
4. 
Ensure timely payments and accelerating disbursement. As the project approaches its 
closing date in two years’ time, disbursement stands at US$122.03 million or around 48%, and 
around 60% committed. Projected disbursement between December 2024 and December 2025 
approximately US$65 million, and payments ready for submission to the Bank by December 2024 
stands at US$8 million. The mission noted there was payment delay in Garowe, Puntland for 
UNOPS. This issue was resolved during the mission. For future payments, similar issue may arise. 
The team recommends for the government led by MPWRH to make an agreement with Puntland 
EAFS for way forward to ensure timely payment. It was agreed during the mission that the PCU 
FM specialist will facilitate monthly check-in with each PIU FM specialist, to ensure timely 
submission of payments.  
 
5. 
Strengthening contract management. Contract management remains an issue. To bolster 
the PIUs capacity around contract management a training was held for PCU and PIUs, prior to the 
MTR. A new mitigation plan was agreed during the mission, which will include joint contract 
management plans and contract registers between the project teams and UNOPS to ensure parties 
meet their obligations. The PCU will continue to convene monthly ‘check-ins’ with each PIU and 
UNOPS to review progress, which will also be informed by monthly progress reports and in the 
Management Information System (MIS).  
 
6. 
Building institutional capacity for sustainability. The mission highlighted the importance 
of further building the capacity at multiple levels of government to support delivery of municipal 
services and for sustainability of investments initiated under the project. This includes federal level 
MPWRH and federal member state (FMS) level, although not overtaking the implementation 
function of the municipal governments.  
 
o Capacity building for FGS and FMS MPWRH will be a joint effort with the Somalia 
Horn of Africa Infrastructure Integration Project (SHIIP) and mobilizing other resources, 
such as the Programmatic Analytical and Advisory Services (PASA) Livable Cities and 
Disaster Resilience Platform.  
o Capacity building for municipal staff. Participating cities through the PIU will prepare a 
proposal for targeted capacity building for operations and maintenance for municipal staff 
and FMS MPWRH engineers, as well as proposed list of equipment and/or tools needed for 
O&M. PCU will facilitate consultation process with FGS and FMS MPWRH for the 
proposed capacity building need. The first consultation will be in the second week of 
December as part of the agenda for monthly check-in.  
o Capacity building for PCU and PIUs. The mission noted there is a need for strengthening 
communications and capacity of PCU and PIUs on project management. Team building and

expected to be shared with the Bank by December 9, 2024.  
 
9. 
Proposed Somalia Informal Settlements Upgrading Project. The World Bank, together 
with MPWRH, the Project Coordination Unit (PCU), Project Implementation Unit (PIU), U.N 
agencies, MOPIED Durable Solutions Unit, discussed technical aspects of project preparation for 
the Somalia Informal Settlement Upgrading Project (SISUP). These discussions were led by 
thematic experts and covered critical areas, including the role of urban upgrading, sites and services 
and rental housing in the housing value chain, settlement typology and selection, options for 
infrastructure and service delivery (including social enterprises), livelihoods and job creation and 
environmental and social frameworks pertinent to urban upgrading and housing.  The proposed 
Project timeline includes a Project Concept Note this Fiscal Year (ending June 2025), followed by 
a targeted project approval in Q1/Q2 next FY (the Bank’s FY starting July 2025).  To facilitate 
Project preparation, the mission discussed with the MPWRH on an updated request letter for the 
Project from the Ministry of Finance and for stakeholder consultations planned for January 2025.   
 
10. 
Programmatic Analytical and Advisory Services (PASA) Livable Cities and Disaster 
Resilience Platform. The mission also introduced the Livable Cities Platform PASA.  Designed 
with an implementation over 3 years, the PASA will undertake analytics and technical assistance 
under 5 pillars.  These include: (i) Disaster and Climate Risk Management; (ii) Inclusive Cities; 
(iii) Urban Services; (iv) Competitive Cities; and (v) Alternative financing for municipal 
governments.  Building on insights from the Somalia Urbanization Review, PASA will enhance 
diagnostic capacity, inform long-term urban strategy, and support the ISU’s policy dialogue and 
preparatory work. As initial engagements, the Client and the Team agreed on the need for Housing 
and Land Policy Dialogue, Settlements and Spatial Analytics in key cities – including a mapping 
of public land in Mogadishu and selected cities.

o Project Development Objective. The PDO of the project is “to strengthen public service 
delivery capacity of local governments, increase access to climate-resilient urban 
infrastructure and services, and to provide immediate and effective response to an eligible 
crisis or emergency in selected areas.” The PDO and its indicators remain highly relevant 
and achievable. The project aims to provide 1 million people with improved access to 
climate-resilient urban infrastructure and services and has already exceeded 1 million 
beneficiaries, of which a significant proportion are women and IDPs.  
o Performance towards achieving PDO and Implementation Progress (IP).  
• 
The progress towards achievement of PDO remains Satisfactory. 
• 
The performance of IP upgraded as Satisfactory. The mission acknowledged 
significant improvement on implementation progress and disbursement. Further, 
the mission acknowledged full compliance of ESF, PCU and PIUs are fully staffed 
and budgeted, three out of four PDO indictors have been achieved, and Component 
4. Urban Forced Displacement successfully completed.  
o Disbursement trend. As of November 18, 2024, cumulative disbursement stands at 
US$122.03 million, representing approximately 48 percent of the total budget of US$253.5 
million with approximately 60 percent committed. The disbursement has increased by 
US$27.36 million in 6 months since the previous ISM in May 2024.  
o The use of country system and performance-based allocation. The mission 
acknowledged that one of the achievements of the project is the use of country system on 
the allocating funds to participating cities. On implementation, cities are responsible for 
implementing the project, including participatory planning and consultations, 
procurement, supervision, and adopting operations and maintenance manual.  
o Institutional strengthening and capacity building. This is key to the success of project 
implementation in Somalia. The project started with capacity building to the PCU and 
PIUs. At MTR, the mission noted that there has been transfer of knowledge and trainings 
to municipal staff and peer learning from other projects.  
o U.N agencies to support project implementation. The project, through the government 
of Somalia, contracted the United Nations Office for Project Services (UNOPS) to support 
the PIU on supervision of civil works. In addition, UNOPS provided design for 
infrastructure investments and capacity building program for the PIUs and municipal staff 
for sustainability. The MTR mission acknowledge the importance of continuing with this 
model throughout the project.

17. 
Progress to Date. Key accomplishments include the development of a geo-referenced 
monitoring system and a web-based Management Information System (MIS), more structured

engagement from the UNOPS and successful PIUs.

Annex 1B: Technical Note on Drainage Investment in Mogadishu 
 
Drainage Investments- Overall 
  
A Multi Criteria Analysis had been carried out to identify priority investment of the project that mainly 
resulted in the identification of selected roads, bridges and drainage investments in the project cities. In 
preparation for drainage investments; the PIU had carried out the necessary feasibility studies, preliminary 
designs, environmental and social impact assessments, detailed engineering designs and bidding 
documents; and these activities remain at various stages of implementation.  
 
The Project Coordination Unit (PCU) had provided observations and recommendation in terms of important 
considerations for modelling and detailed design of these infrastructure investments. It is expected that 
these observations and recommendation are deliberated by UNOPS and associated design teams prior to 
finalization of the detailed designs; notwithstanding, the final design responsibility rest with the UNOPS 
and necessary technical rigor is expected. It is important to restore existing drainage systems to the extent 
possible as a way of initial alleviation of flooding. Once restored, an effective plan of action is to be 
deployed for continued regular maintenance.   
 
The Municipalities must endeavor to preserve flood detention and retention areas as much as feasible and 
maintain the natural drainage paths. Secondary to flood reduction, they form important public spaces for 
recreation and greening the surroundings.  
 
Implementation of most drainage packages will commence between March and July 2025; with an effective 
construction period of between 18-24 months. Once contract works commence, given the limited contractor 
capacity and complex nature of drainage engineering; the PCU/PIUs are advised to monitor progress 
diligently in close collaboration with UNOPS. With the asphalting of previously gravel earth roads, increase 
in road foundation height and the introduction of new roadside drainage, increase of runoff is expected that 
could lead to local level ponding. It is therefore important that adequate cross culverts and leader drains are 
incorporated in the road design to prevent increased local flooding. 
  
Drainage Investments in Mogadishu 
  
The Master Plan for the Trunk Drainage in Mogadishu had been completed. After much deliberation, the 
flood infrastructure is proposed to be designed to accommodate a 25-year return period rainfall. The trunk 
drainage infrastructure required to drain such a storm consists of trunk mains of 4m diameter. The proposed 
construction methods for these include open cut sections of depth up to 8m and micro tunnelling. The 
expected cost of the trunk drainage network is USD 446 Million. The practicality of constructing drainage 
mains of 4m diameter using the construction methods proposed in a highly dense city; and the economic 
viability of the works proposed is to be further verified. Additionally, it was noted with concern that the 
existing drainage network in the city had not been included in the model; in effect ignoring its functionality. 
However, the BRA is undertaking a condition assessment of 6.7kms of the estimated 9.0km of the existing 
system network; and this work is to be expanded to cover the full 9.0km system. This report with the cross-
sections, invert levels, manhole shaft locations etc. is to be shared with UNOPS by November 15, 2024, for 
inclusion in the modelling. The PCU/ PIU requested that modeling for return period of 15 and 10 years be 
done for informed decision making as with regards to return period to be used in the detailed engineering 
design.

Annex 3: MTR Assessment on Project Design 
 
Relevance. The PDO of the project is “to strengthen public service delivery capacity of local governments, 
increase access to climate-resilient urban infrastructure and services, and to provide immediate and 
effective response to an eligible crisis or emergency in selected areas.” The PDO and its indicators remain 
highly relevant and achievable and should be retained. The project aims to provide 1 million people with 
improved access to climate-resilient urban infrastructure and services and has already exceeded 1 million 
beneficiaries reached under the municipal drought response, of which a significant proportion are women 
and IDPs.  
 
The relevance for SURP-II remains high particularly in light of the El Niño Southern Oscillation 
(ENSO), which influences Somalia’s climate variability, bringing more rainfall and flooding during El 
Niño and droughts in La Niña years.23 Climate-related shocks are occurring in increasingly quick 
succession, leaving little time for Somalis to recover.24 During the MTR, the SURP-II mayors highlighted 
the significance of the project for increasing urban resilience to climate and disaster risks, especially 
flooding through drainage infrastructure investments and analytics that have helped prioritize Flood Risk 
Mitigation (FRM) investments. The mayors expressed demand for further support to strengthen urban 
resilience, including improving the capacity of municipal land administration departments and increasing 
the skills of local government functions on urban planning.  
 
Expected outputs and results of the project, particularly the PDO indicators are gaining momentum. 
Three of the four PDO-level indicators are either on track or already surpassed. The only indicator that has 
been lagging is on total people with improved access to climate-resilient urban infrastructure and services. 
Beneficiaries are only counted when a sub-project has been completely handed over. The target for this 
indicator was substantially increased with the third additional financing to factor in additional sub-projects 
that had not been included in the original project. As of the latest estimates, 489,899 people have benefitted 
from completed works, which stands at 49% of the end target of 1,000,000. Given the scale of works 
ongoing, under procurement and in the pipeline, the indicator is expected to be achieved by project closing 
date. 
 
Achievements  
 
At the time of the MTR, the project has accomplished the following:  
 
1. Three additional financing, with total project amount of US$253.5m. The project has pioneered 
approaches like municipal-led implementation, use of country system, inter-governmental fund 
transfer, participatory planning, economic inclusion, capacity building and analytical works that 
have tangibly strengthened the public service delivery capacity of local governments while

o Open Drains as Safety Hazards: Although the new drainage systems have been effective

community members recommending that they be covered to prevent accidents. 
 
• 
Lessons for Future Projects:

purpose. Ongoing consultations helped build trust and align project goals with community 
needs. 
o Effective Management of Road Closures: Enhanced coordination with local businesses 
and public services during road closures could mitigate the impact of disruptions, allowing 
better preparation for alternative routes and access points. 
o Regular Maintenance Planning: Establishing maintenance plans for drainage and road 
infrastructure during the planning stages helps ensure long-term project sustainability, 
minimizing the risk of deterioration over time.
