Figures are net of additionality: the part that would happen anyway without the policy is removed.

**Meat.** The plants ship about 19,200 t of chilled beef and camel meat in 2032. That is about 12% of the 156,599 t the six GCC states imported in 2023 (D-0063), Net of sales that would happen anyway, exports are worth USD 84.6 million, about 8% of the market's value, because the model prices Somali meat near Pakistan's, not at the Gulf average. The high case reaches about a fifth. Proximity is the case: chilled meat keeps for weeks, not months, and Mogadishu is days by sea and hours by air from Gulf ports. Australian and South American suppliers are weeks away. The main risks are:
- Gulf import approvals and disease-free zones.
- Lean zebu carcasses selling below premium cuts, which is why the price is set at 90% of the Gulf average.
- Competition with the live trade for animals. The plants need about 12.5% of current cattle and camel offtake (slaughter plus live exports, carcass weight). Diverted animals take about 14% of live exports.

**What the Gulf's suppliers show** (UN Comtrade 2023, S-156):
- **Pakistan.** It supplied 80,499 t, 37% of Gulf chilled beef imports by value (D-0064, D-0065), at USD 5.02/kg (D-0066). A nearby halal supplier of lean carcass beef can take a large share. That is the model for Somalia, and Pakistan is also the main competitor.
- **Australia and Brazil.** They follow with 19% and 14%.
- **Ethiopia and Kenya.** Together they hold under 3%, though Ethiopia's small trade fetches about USD 10/kg in Saudi Arabia (D-0067).
- **The price I use.** I have priced Somali meat at USD 5.50/kg, just above Pakistan, instead of 90% of the Gulf average. The lower price trims the meat line's value but makes it defensible.

**Frankincense and myrrh.** The case is real but small, and the policy should say so:
- **What exists.** Production was worth about USD 80 to 90 million a year before the drought (C-0413). It was Somalia's second-largest export after 1991 (C-0433), yet it leaves as raw lumps (C-0414).
- **The limit on value.** A distillery yields oil equal to about 5.5% of the resin by weight (C-0418). FAO judged that processing at origin offers the possibility of "modest" foreign-exchange gains, because oil volumes are small (C-0417).
- **What buyers pay now.** France and Germany paid about USD 11.44/kg for Somali gums and resins in 2023 (D-0068). Somalia's small essential-oil exports fetched about USD 200/kg (D-0069). The eleven partner countries that reported recorded only USD 11.0 million of Somali gum and resin imports in 2023, a code that also covers gum arabic (D-0070), against the Export Strategy's USD 80 to 90 million production value, so much trade is unrecorded or passes through the UAE.
- **Where the money is.** Most of the value lies in grading, cleaning, certification (organic, sustainable harvest), traceable supply and extracts for perfumery and pharmaceuticals. Oil is only part of it.
- **What to say about sourcing.** The resin comes from Sanaag and Bari, so the facility needs supply agreements with producers there. The chapter should state this neutrally.

**Clean cooking as manufacturing.** The technology needs assessment supports it:
- Stoves retail at about USD 50 (C-0419), but households can afford about USD 10 (C-0420).
- Most stoves are imported, often from Kenya (C-0421).
- The Jikokoa stove cuts charcoal use by 64% (C-0422).
- The NDC already calls for biomass briquettes from agricultural waste (C-0423).

The policy offers manufacturers park land, duty relief on machinery and a purchase subsidy. The subsidy covers the gap between the USD 40 factory price and the USD 10 households can pay, funded by the voluntary carbon market. In the base case it costs about USD 2.8 million in 2032, for about 218,000 additional efficient stoves in use.

One caution for the financing chapter. Stoves sold in Mogadishu earn no carbon credits under the current CDM default (Banaadir fNRB of zero, C-0169). Of the 161,100 t of CO2 the stoves avoid in 2032, only about 64,400 t is creditable (the pellets avoid another 12,800 t). Carbon finance should therefore target stoves sold outside Banaadir.

**Pellets.** They mostly replace charcoal, not imports, so part of their jobs and income replaces charcoal making. The model counts only 40% as net new, which leaves their GDP contribution small. Their case rests on protecting woodland, clearing invasive Prosopis, cutting emissions and lowering household fuel bills.

## 4. How I set the assumptions

You delegated the assumptions. I set each one from the best benchmark available and recorded your delegation in its `approval` field in `data/assumptions.yaml`. The benchmarks are:
- IFPRI's social accounting matrices for value-added ratios.
- IMF fiscal and balance-of-payments tables for the customs rate.
- UN Comtrade for Gulf prices and market size.
- The Central Bank and FAOSTAT for livestock supply.
- Plant disclosures for jobs and capital cost.

Where no benchmark exists, the rationale says so. You can amend any entry, and the model reruns in a minute.

The results move most with these assumptions:
- BPO seats and revenue per seat.
- Fish processing capacity and price.
- Garment jobs.
- Meat capacity.
- Additionality.
- The build schedule.

These are policy targets as much as assumptions. Part 2 of the policy will set its 2032 targets from them, so they are the ones to review if you review any.
