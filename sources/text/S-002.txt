Executive Summary 
Introduction 
The National Transformation Plan (NTP 2025-2029) is a ground-breaking, inspirational, and forward-
looking document that will propel Somalia into a promising future. The plan envisions a Somalia with 
stable and inclusive governance, anchored on the rule of law, and a thriving, sustainable economy that 
can provide high-quality services and contribute to the well-being of its citizens. The NTP is centred 
around four key pillars, which are: 
 
1. Transformational Governance 
2. Sustainable Economic Transformation 
3. Social and Human Capital Transformation  
4. Environment and Climate Resilience 
 
Goals and Objectives of the NTP 
Being a medium-term blueprint for an economically vibrant, socially thriving, environmentally 
resilient, and democratically stable Somalia, the development of the NTP is motivated by the collective 
aspirations for a better and prosperous Somalia as the country progresses towards sustainable 
transformation. 
 
The NTP aligns with Somalia’s Centennial Vision 2060: “to create a prosperous, secure, democratic, 
inclusive, and competitive country with a high-quality life”. In pursuit of that vision, the goal of the 
NTP is to consolidate peace and create sustainable economic growth and improved social well-being 
by achieving the following core objectives: 
 
● Establish and strengthen institutions for transparent, accountable, and inclusive governance. 
● Foster macroeconomic stability and lay foundations for the diversification of the economy. 
● Build critical infrastructure for sustainable development, including roads, energy, public 
buildings, and broadband capability. 
● Increase support to the social sector for human capital development and protect the vulnerable 
population, particularly persons with disabilities. 
● Increase resilience to environmental and climate shocks 
● Mainstream gender equity, human rights, and inclusivity in all development policies and 
programs by empowering women, youth and People with Disability as drivers of growth and 
nation-building. 
 
Transition from NDP to NTP 
Since it gained independence in 1960, Somalia developed nine National Development Plans (NDPs); 
of these, seven were developed prior to the collapse of the central government, while the remaining 
two were drafted after 2012 during the period of recovery and state building. The lessons learned from 
these previous NDPs revealed that the anticipated level of development was not achieved. In a country 
like Somalia that is recovering from decades of civil and political unrest, a complete paradigm shift

leading to huge transformation in all sectors is deemed necessary. The important progress made under 
NDP-9 highlighted the need for a comprehensive transformation across all sectors to address 
persistent challenges and unlock the country’s full potential. 
 
In response, the government recognized the urgency of shifting from the traditional development-
oriented framework to a National Transformation Plan (NTP). This strategic shift aims to accelerate 
the development of Somalia’s socioeconomic sectors by implementing targeted, high-impact 
interventions. The NTP is designed to drive real and tangible growth across all sectors, fostering 
economic stability, enhancing social services, and improving the overall quality of life for the Somali 
people. 
 
Approach and Methodology  
Approach 
The formulation of the National Transformation Plan (NTP) incorporated an inclusive and 
participatory process by engaging stakeholders across the country. This approach integrates diverse 
perspectives, fosters ownership, and aligns with national priorities by incorporating both top-down 
and bottom-up mechanisms. Communication played a central role, with a dedicated team utilizing 
platforms like TV, radio, and social media to maximize public awareness and participation. Stakeholder 
consultations targeted a wide range of groups, including government entities, the private sector, civil 
society, international partners, academia, and the diaspora. 
 
The process focused on sectoral discussions at federal and state levels. Consultations conducted 
gathered inputs on priorities and challenges to establish the foundations of the NTP formulation. 
 
Methodology 
The NTP development process utilized a mixed-method approach combining quantitative and 
qualitative methods to provide a holistic understanding of Somalia’s key economic, social, governance, 
and environmental challenges and sectoral priorities. The methodology comprised four phases: the 
pre-lab, lab, and post-lab stages, and FMS consultations. 
 
Pre-Lab Phase 
The pre-lab phase involved desk reviews and stakeholder engagements, examining existing data from 
baseline documents, institutional strategic plans, prior national development plans, sector-specific 
reports, assessments, and statistical reports. 
 
Numerous stakeholders, including ministries, departments, and agencies (MDAs); the private sector; 
civil society organizations; the UN; and development partners, participated in the pre-lab phase. 
 
Federal Member State (FMS) Consultations 
The Ministry of Planning, Investment, and Economic Development (MoPIED) held consultations 
with the Federal Member States (FMS) between July to December 2024 to identify key states’ 
opportunities, challenges, and priorities. These consultations provided an opportunity to align states’ 
priorities with the National Transformation Plan’s sectoral priorities.

The NTP technical teams facilitated inclusive discussions and active engagement, allowing participants 
to articulate challenges, propose solutions, and align their priorities with national objectives. Key

Lab Phase 
The Lab Phase encompassed sectoral labs across the four pillars, including economics, social

Post-Lab Phase 
At the conclusion of the lab phase, all outcomes and deliberation from the lab reports were synthesized

implementation strategy and a robust monitoring framework.

Validation sessions involving relevant stakeholders have been conducted to validate the NTP 
document, enhance ownership, gather feedback, and prepare for its implementation. 
 
Key Pillar Findings  
Transformational Governance 
Transformational governance is the foundational base for a peaceful, democratic, and stable Somalia, 
and it encompasses inclusive politics, security, rule of law, and public sector reform. 
 
Inclusive Politics 
The Inclusive Politics pillar is a cornerstone of Somalia's state-building journey. It aligns with the 
Capable State pillar of Somalia’s Centennial Vision 2060, which aims to address state fragility and 
foster a peaceful, democratic Somalia. The Inclusive Politics pillar consists of four key initiatives: 
Political Stability, National Cohesion, Local Government Development, and strengthening 
International Cooperation and Diplomacy. 
 
The Political Stability Initiative tackles key challenges in federalism, including the completion of the 
constitution, the unbundling of functional assignments, and the development of an effective 
Intergovernmental Relations (IGR) framework. Additionally, it promotes democratization by 
advancing free and fair elections at all levels of government, ensuring the 30% women’s quota, and 
enhancing the political participation of marginalized groups, including youth and people with 
disabilities.

The adoption of the 2012 Provisional Constitution introduced federalism as Somalia's governance 
model, providing a framework for decentralization, state-building, and the formation of Federal 
Member States (FMSs). To establish a robust and inclusive federal structure, the Federal Government 
of Somalia (FGS) has undertaken key reforms, including the unbundling of government functions, 
finalization of the constitution, recognition of local governance structures, and strengthening of 
intergovernmental relations (IGR). 
 
A significant milestone in Somalia’s constitutional reform process was reached in March 2024, when 
Parliament approved amendments to the first four chapters of the Provisional Constitution. This 
review process emphasizes national dialogue, inclusivity, and consensus-building, with the goal of 
finalizing the constitution by 2026. 
 
As Somalia transitions towards democratization and good governance, electoral reforms remain a 
priority. In November 2024, Parliament approved critical legislation, including the Political Parties 
Act, Electoral System Act, and the National Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission 
(NIEBC) Act. Efforts are also underway to increase women’s representation in Parliament from 
12.33% to 30% and ensure the political inclusion of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and persons 
with disabilities. 
 
The second initiative under Inclusive Politics focuses on strengthening local governance to ensure 
efficient service delivery. While local governments are tasked with providing healthcare, education, 
and infrastructure, they often face public distrust and inadequate capacity and resources to deliver 
services. The National Transformation Plan (NTP) underscores the importance of devolving fiscal 
and administrative powers alongside capacity-building measures to enable local governments to 
function effectively. To address the capacity gaps, institutions such as the National Institute of Local 
Government (NILG) play a crucial role in equipping local authorities with the skills and knowledge 
needed to manage their responsibilities. By strengthening local governance, Somalia can enhance 
public trust, accountability, and equitable access to services, ensuring that decentralization benefits all 
communities. 
 
The third initiative under Inclusive Politics is National Cohesion, which focuses on fostering unity 
through grassroots reconciliation efforts and inclusive dialogues that engage diverse stakeholders, 
including marginalized groups and communities of newly liberated areas. This initiative seeks to 
address both social and political reconciliation, ensuring that historical grievances are acknowledged 
and resolved through structured and participatory processes. 
 
Guided by the National Reconciliation Framework (NRF), this initiative outlines key interventions to 
strengthen social harmony, trust and national unity. By fostering dialogue and inclusive engagement, 
Somalia can build a more cohesive and resilient society. 
 
The fourth and final initiative covers the diplomatic and internation cooperation front, which Somalia 
has made significant progress in strengthening regional and international engagements to advance its 
national interests. As a member of key organizations such as the United Nations (UN), the African 
Union (AU), the Arab League, and the East African Community (EAC), Somalia has access to 
platforms that enable it to expand its political, economic, and social influence.

By fulfilling its financial and regulatory obligations, Somalia will be able to fully capitalize on the 
membership opportunities available. This ensures active participation, access to funding, and technical 
support. Additionally, completing the EAC Roadmap for regional integration is essential for

A crucial aspect of Somalia’s diplomatic strategy is diaspora inclusion, leveraging the contributions of

Additionally, enhancing institutional capacity is vital to these efforts, which requires expanding the

Through these four key initiatives, the Inclusive Politics pillar plays a fundamental role in Somalia’s 
state-building efforts, fostering political stability, social cohesion, effective governance, and

Security 
 
Developing Somalia security sector focuses on enhancing the capacity and professionalism of federal 
and state security institutions, including the Somali National Armed Forces (SNAF), Somali Police 
Force (SPF), custodial corps, and the National Intelligence and Security Agency (NISA). The Ministry 
of Defense plays a central role in strengthening the SNAF, improving recruitment, training, and 
operational effectiveness. The Ministry of Internal Security oversees law enforcement, 
counterterrorism, and internal stability, ensuring police, intelligence services, immigration and border 
control can effectively prevent and respond to threats. 
 
Security sector development is a top priority for the government, focusing on legal frameworks, 
capabilities development, institutional restructuring, and sustainable funding mechanisms.  
 
Counterterrorism efforts so far target Al-Shabaab and ISIS, while border security, arms control, and 
maritime security are key to safeguarding Somalia’s territorial integrity. Regional and international 
partnerships are vital for the government’s efforts, including in finances, capacity building and 
intelligence-sharing. 
 
The National Security Architecture (NSArch), adopted in 2023, aligns federal and state security efforts, 
emphasizing collaboration, integration of Somali Security Forces (SSF), and transitioning to a Somali-
led security framework. The Somali Security Sector Development Plan (SSDP), launched in December 
2023, identifies capability gaps and outlines a framework for achieving sustainable security prioritized 
in phased approach. The ultimate goal is for SSF to assume full responsibility for Somalia’s security 
by the end of the current mandate of African Union Support and Stabilization Mission (AUSSOM).

Rule of law:  
At this critical juncture in its recovery from decades of conflict and political instability, Somalia's 
national development priorities heavily rely on the justice sector. The ongoing reform efforts have 
been guided by the need to build institutions that enable access to justice, promote the rule of law, 
and address long-standing grievances. By strengthening the capacity of rule of law institutions and 
building a more consistent culture of accountability, Somalia aims to break cycles of lawlessness while 
restoring public confidence in its justice system. 
  
Institutional integrity is essential for reducing impunity and promoting accountability. All rule of law 
institutions must be independent, free from corruption, and empowered to enforce the law with 
transparency. This effort will help restore societal trust in the justice system and foster an environment 
where fairness and accountability can thrive 
The key priorities of the Rule of Law emphasize expanding access to justice, restoring and 
strengthening the core functions of rule of law institutions, and strengthening accountability 
mechanisms. 
 
To address these challenges, the ministry of justice has initiated comprehensive reforms aimed at 
strengthening the judiciary, expanding access to justice, modernizing correctional services, and 
enhancing human rights protections). The Ministry of Justice plays a central role in this process, 
overseeing the development and harmonization of Somalia’s justice system to ensure fairness, 
accessibility, and efficiency. 
 
A key priority under the NTP is the establishment and strengthening of core judicial functions to build 
a more independent and effective judiciary. This includes setting up a fully functional High Judicial 
Council, constitutional courts, and operationalizing the National Anti-Corruption Commission to 
improve accountability and transparency. Additionally, the plan emphasizes the modernization of 
corrections services, the establishment of a Law Reform Commission to ensure legal consistency and 
standardization. The digitization of justice services, along with the automation of case management 
systems, is expected to enhance efficiency and accessibility, particularly in a country where judicial 
infrastructure remains weak. Furthermore, expanding the reach of formal courts, legal aid services, 
and mobile courts in newly recovered areas is essential to ensuring that justice is not only available but 
also equitable, particularly for displaced populations and vulnerable communities.

The NTP also prioritizes reforms in the correctional sector to align Somalia’s prison system with 
international standards, focusing on improving the treatment of detainees, especially women, 
juveniles, and radicalized individuals. Key initiatives include rehabilitating correctional facilities, 
ensuring humane living conditions, and establishing comprehensive rehabilitation programs that 
integrate vocational training, education, and mental health services. Strengthening oversight 
mechanisms through regular monitoring and evaluation is crucial to maintaining transparency and 
accountability. Additionally, ongoing efforts to define the Custodial Corps as a civilian service provider 
rather than a security force will play a vital role in professionalizing prison services and shifting the 
focus from static security to rehabilitation and reintegration. 
 
Finally, the protection and promotion of human rights remain integral to the justice sector reforms. 
Establishing institutions such as the Human Rights Commission and Truth Commission will help 
address historical grievances and conflict-related land and property disputes. National mechanisms for 
human rights implementation, reporting, and follow-up will be strengthened to ensure compliance 
with international obligations. Capacity-building programs for law enforcement, human rights 
activists, and judicial personnel will enhance Somalia’s ability to uphold justice and human rights 
effectively. Furthermore, public awareness campaigns and transitional justice mechanisms will foster 
societal engagement and reconciliation, ensuring that legal reforms are not only institutional but also 
deeply embedded in the social fabric of the nation. These reforms, collectively, aim to create a robust, 
fair, and modern justice system that supports Somalia’s long-term stability and development. 
 
 
Public Sector Reform 
 
Public Sector Reform refers to the deliberate efforts to improve the efficiency, effectiveness, 
accountability, and responsiveness of government institutions and processes. These reforms aim to 
enhance public service delivery, promote good governance, and foster economic development by 
aligning government functions with the needs of citizens and the broader socio-economic 
environment. Public sector reform focuses on the following Key Result Areas: 
 
Improving Public Service & Civil Servant Management 
Reforming civil service management is crucial for professionalizing the workforce and establishing a 
merit-based civil service capable of delivering efficient and equitable services. According to the World 
Bank’s Government Effectiveness Index, which measures perceptions of public service quality, the 
civil service's independence from political influence, the effectiveness of policy formulation and 
implementation, and the government's commitment to these policies, Somalia ranks among the lowest, 
with an index score of -2.03 (2022). This score places Somalia significantly behind both the East 
African region and global benchmarks, highlighting the urgency of comprehensive public sector 
reforms. 
 
This Key Result Area focuses the following priority initiatives: (i) organizational re-structuring and 
modernization of HR Business Process (ii) civil service capacity development through training 
program aligned with the required competences and (iii) establishment of facilities and istitutional 
infrastructures for a Civil Service Training Institute and for NCSC. Together, these initiatives drive

Institutional Integrity and Accountability 
Strengthening governance structures and fostering a culture of transparency lie at the heart of efforts 
to restore institutional integrity. However, institutional fragility, weak enforcement mechanisms, and 
systemic corruption undermine progress. Many public institutions struggle to effectively execute and 
maintain reforms, with legal frameworks often outdated and inadequate for addressing contemporary 
governance challenges.  Additionally, limited collaboration among integrity institutions and inadequate 
whistle-blower protections perpetuates a culture of impunity and erodes public trust. 
 
Corruption in Somalia remains a critical challenge, as evidenced by its ranking on the Transparency 
International Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI). In 2023, Somalia scored 11 out of 100 on the CPI, 
making it the most corrupt country out of 180 countries assessed. This low score highlights severe 
issues in public sector corruption, including a lack of accountability and barriers to justice.  
 
This Key Result Area focuses the following priority initiatives: (i) establish independent integrity 
institutions, (ii) enhance Citizen-Centric Governance through Transparency & Accountability nd (iii) 
Strengthen the Capacity of Integrity Institutions and Promote Professionalism. Together, foster 
accountability, combat corruption, and enhance public trust through empowered institutions and 
participatory governance. 
 
Digital Transformation and E-Government

Somalia's current digital landscape remains underdeveloped, characterized by nascent digital 
infrastructure, limited e-government services, and a fragmented approach to digital transformation.

national digital strategy to bridge these gaps. 
 
As Somalia continues to build its public sector infrastructure, e-governance presents an opportunity 
to modernize and streamline government processes. In many African countries, such as Rwanda and 
Kenya, digital platforms for service delivery have significantly enhanced efficiency and accessibility, 
particularly for remote and underserved populations. However, Somalia's limited digital infrastructure 
and low internet penetration pose substantial challenges to the widespread adoption of e-governance. 
Addressing these challenges requires targeted investments in ICT infrastructure, capacity building, and 
policy frameworks to create a robust foundation for digital transformation.

Sustainable Economic Development Pillar  
The Sustainable Economic Development Pillar of Somalia's National Transformation Plan 
(NTP; 2025-2029) is crucial for revitalizing key sectors, including agriculture, livestock, fisheries, 
infrastructure, and private businesses through targeted reforms and macroeconomic strategies.  
Driving productivity in agriculture and livestock  
The National Transformation Plan (NTP I) prioritizes modernizing agriculture and livestock 
production to enhance food security, market competitiveness, and export diversification. Over 
the next five years, the focus will be on irrigation expansion, climate-smart farming, value chain 
development, and post-harvest infrastructure to drive higher yields and sustainable productivity. 
By 2029, key agricultural targets include: 
 Cereal production increasing by 127.3 kg/ha, with 300 farmers trained in modern techniques. 
 Seed production expanding by 68%, reaching 250 MT in annual sales. 
 Fodder production achieving 270 tonnes annually to enhance feed security. 
 High-value crops, fruits, and vegetables reaching 375 tonnes annually to boost exports. 
 Poultry production scaling up to 86 million eggs per year, reducing import dependency. 
For livestock, Somalia aims to: 
 Increase meat exports, processing 921,300 animals annually through modernized facilities. 
 Expand dairy production, reaching 7,900 liters of fresh milk daily through value-added 
processing. 
 Enhance animal health and genetics, improving treatment and breeding for 1.77 million 
animals. 
 
To strengthen climate resilience, investments in irrigation, drought mitigation, and sustainable 
land management will benefit 2 million people across 15+ regions. New agricultural 
marketplaces in Afgoye and Balad will improve trade efficiency, reducing post-harvest losses and 
enhancing farmer incomes. 
 
Somalia is also investing in research, pest management, and regulatory services to drive 
innovation and ensure long-term agricultural sustainability, positioning the sector for higher 
productivity, export growth, and economic resilience. 
 
Fisheries Sector  
 
Somalia's fisheries sector is a key pillar of economic growth, food security, and job creation. The 
National Transformation Plan (NTP I) prioritizes expanding fishing ports, enhancing processing and 
cold-chain infrastructure, and strengthening monitoring systems to combat illegal fishing and boost 
sustainable production.

By 2029, fish production is set to double from 120,000 to 240,000 metric tons (MT), creating over 
23,800 new jobs. Investments in modern fishing fleets, sustainable gear, mariculture, and cold storage 
will improve efficiency and reduce post-harvest losses. Upgrading ports in Mogadishu, Berbera, and 
Kismayo will facilitate industry growth and increase exports by 40%. 
To increase household fish consumption from 3 kg to 5 kg per capita, the government is strengthening 
fish processing, expanding distribution networks, and establishing a Fishery & Marine Resource 
Institute to develop expertise. Public-private partnerships will drive value-added processing and fish 
oil production, contributing $98.8M in investments and generating 5,630 additional jobs. 
Major initiatives include Secure Economic Zones (SEZs) by 2027, Project Badmaal ($45M investment) 
to modernize fishing communities and enhanced surveillance systems targeting 30 vessel detentions 
by 2029. These efforts will diversify fish supply, stabilize production, and reinforce Somalia’s position 
as a regional leader in sustainable fisheries. 
 
Tourism Sector 
 Revitalizing Somalia’s Tourism Sector: Somalia is prioritizing security, infrastructure, and 
international marketing to position itself as a cultural and eco-tourism destination. The goal is to attract 
50,000–75,000 visitors annually by 2029, driving economic growth and global recognition. 
To achieve this, Somalia is enhancing tourism security and policies, establishing visitor tracking 
systems and centralized tourism databases to improve safety and market analysis. Cultural and eco-
tourism development is a key focus, with efforts to rehabilitate heritage sites, foster public-private 
partnerships, and position Somalia as a regional tourism hub. 
Investments in workforce development and infrastructure will equip 10,000 hospitality professionals 
annually while improving critical tourism facilities and research programs. Eco-tourism hubs, marine 
conservation projects, and national branding campaigns like "The Beauty of Africa" will showcase 
Somalia’s rich cultural and natural heritage.

unlocking natural resources, and strengthening governance. By 2029, these efforts aim to attract $5.3 
billion in private investment, create 15,000 jobs, cut electricity tariffs by 50%, and add $1.45 billion to 
GDP, while advancing renewable energy and climate resilience.

Macroeconomic Stability  
Focuses on delivering public finance reforms and enhancing financial services.Somalia is advancing 
fiscal sustainability and economic resilience through public finance reforms, revenue mobilization, and 
financial sector modernization. The National Transformation Plan (NTP I) prioritizes strengthening 
fiscal management, fostering financial inclusion, and enabling private-sector growth to reduce donor 
dependence and attract investment. 
To achieve this, the government is enhancing Public Finance Management (PFM) by improving 
budget planning, expenditure control, and debt management to ensure post-HIPC fiscal stability. 
Expanding the tax base through digital revenue collection and modernized tariffs will boost domestic 
resources, while National Zakat & Waqf Systems will support self-sufficiency. 
Public-private partnerships (PPPs) will drive infrastructure investment, while improved aid 
management and financial inclusion initiatives will optimize resource allocation. Modernizing financial 
markets through the reintroduction of the Somali Shilling, digital banking, and regulatory reforms will 
enhance monetary stability and investor confidence. 
 
Infrastructure And Logistics Sectors 
 
Covering roads, seaports, aviation, and ICT—are key to economic growth, trade competitiveness, 
and regional integration. Years of conflict and limited investment have left these sectors

Key targets include Rehabilitating 3,825 km of roads and constructing 1,963 km of new trade

TEU to 1,520,000 TEU of cargo by 2029 with three new deep-water ports in Mogadishu, Barawe,

Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSME)  
are vital to Somalia’s economic growth, job creation, and poverty reduction, yet they face significant 
barriers such as limited access to finance, weak infrastructure, and regulatory inefficiencies. The 
National Transformation Plan (NTP I) prioritizes strengthening MSMEs by formalizing businesses, 
enhancing financial inclusion, expanding market access, and fostering innovation-driven private sector 
growth. 
To achieve this, Somalia is focusing on creating a supportive business environment, improving access 
to finance, building capacity for innovation, expanding market reach, and advancing policy reforms. 
Strengthening regulatory frameworks, digitalizing business registration, and enhancing infrastructure 
will improve MSME competitiveness, while targeted financial products, credit guarantees, and digital 
finance solutions will unlock capital for growth. Capacity-building initiatives will equip entrepreneurs 
with skills in digital transformation, e-commerce, and innovation, ensuring long-term sustainability. 
Expanding market access through e-commerce, modernized customs, and industrial clusters will 
enable MSMEs to compete globally, while legal and institutional reforms will formalize businesses, 
strengthen trade policies, and integrate MSMEs into regional and global value chains. 
By aligning these efforts with NTP I priorities, Somalia aims to remove structural barriers, accelerate 
private sector-led growth, and position MSMEs as key drivers of economic transformation and 
resilience. 
Employment And Labour Development in Somalia 
Somalia’s labour market faces high unemployment rates, particularly among youth (30.1%) and 
women (40.8%), with limited formal job opportunities, skill mismatches, and weak regulatory 
frameworks. To address these challenges, the National Transformation Plan (NTP I) prioritizes 
employment creation, skills development, and labour market reforms to drive inclusive and sustainable 
job growth.

To bridge employment gaps and create decent work, efforts will focus on establishing Employment 
Service Centres, launching youth employment funds, and fostering private-sector partnerships to

Skills development and employment matching will be enhanced through the development of a Labour 
Market Information System (LMIS) to track demand trends, align vocational training with market

Trade development and global inclusion. The focus areas under this dimension include modernizing

education and training (TVET) and higher education enrollment by 86% will be supported by the 
construction of 3 new Somali National University campuses, the rehabilitation of Somali National 
University (Mogadishu), and the establishment of 12 new TVET centers. The government also aims

Health care 
The Somali government is committed to transforming healthcare under National Transformation Plan 
I (NTP I) to ensure quality, equitable, and resilient health services. A key priority is reducing maternal, 
infant, and child mortality by 50% by 2029 through expanded RMNCAH services, immunization, and 
nutrition programs. 
To strengthen the health workforce, 30,506 professionals will be recruited, including 5,000 female 
community health workers (FCHW), while specialized training programs in emergency medicine, 
oncology, hematology, and cardiology will be introduced. The National Health Professionals Council 
(NHPC) will regulate professional licensing, accreditation, and facility standards. Health system 
resilience will be enhanced by upgrading 18 regional hospitals, establishing 8 state referral hospitals, 
and fostering public-private partnerships to reduce reliance on pharmaceutical imports. Sustainable 
healthcare financing will be achieved by increasing health insurance coverage to 35% by 2029, reducing 
out-of-pocket costs, and leveraging private sector investments. 
Digital health solutions, including a national electronic health records (EHR) system and telemedicine 
services, strengthened monitoring and evaluation (M&E) frameworks will support data-driven 
decision-making. 
The National Medicine Regulatory Authority (NMRA) will be reinforced to ensure the safety, efficacy, 
and quality of medicines and health products, while the National Health Institute will be strengthened 
for emergency preparedness and public health response. 
Through these strategic initiatives, Somalia is on course to achieve Universal Health Coverage (UHC) 
by 2029, ensuring 40% of citizens access affordable, high-quality healthcare.

Water Resource Management  
Access to clean and affordable water is vital for Somalia’s stability and economic growth, yet the 
country faces severe constraints, with only 500 out of 8,305 water sources functional. Challenges such 
as seasonal variability, high costs, and inadequate infrastructure further exacerbate water scarcity. In 
response, the government aims to rehabilitate 2,000 water sources, expand solar-powered water 
systems, and enhance irrigation networks to ensure sustainable and equitable access. Under National 
Transformation Plan I (NTP I), water coverage is set to increase from 71% to 91% by 2029, benefiting 
5 million people through upgraded urban supply systems, climate-resilient rural infrastructure, and 
groundwater monitoring. 
To improve water quality, Somalia will establish a National Central Water Laboratory, introduce small-
scale treatment and desalination plants, and strengthen water monitoring systems to meet international 
standards and reduce waterborne diseases. Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) will be 
reinforced through regulatory reforms, deep groundwater exploration, and riverine restoration 
projects to mitigate floods and support agriculture. Modern telemetry systems will enhance monitoring 
and data-driven decision-making. The success of these initiatives relies on strong governance, 
sustainable financing, public-private partnerships, community participation, and regional water 
cooperation. By 2029, these efforts will transform Somalia’s water sector, ensuring equitable access, 
improved quality, and long-term resilience, ultimately supporting climate adaptation, economic 
development, and national stability.

Environment and Climate Resilience 
The Environment and Climate Resilience Pillar of Somalia’s NTP I serves as a key framework for 
addressing the country’s pressing environmental and climate-related challenges. It comprises two main 
components: Green Growth and Disaster Risk Reduction. 
Green Growth 
The Green Growth chapter outlines Somalia’s transformative journey toward sustainable 
development, emphasizing environmental stewardship while balancing economic progress. Key 
strategic priorities include ecosystem restoration and biodiversity conservation, such as restoring 
15,000 hectares of degraded land, planting 10 million drought-resistant trees, and sequestering 200,000 
metric tons of CO₂. Additional efforts will revitalize three national parks, 10 protected areas, and five 
wildlife sanctuaries, creating 500 jobs and enhancing biodiversity. The Charcoal Reduction 
Program will support reforestation and environmental regeneration. 
Climate adaptation and resource management initiatives will promote sustainable resource use, reduce 
environmental vulnerabilities, and facilitate alternative livelihoods by supporting 50,000 families in 
transitioning to sustainable income sources over the next five years, with a 20% increase in alternative 
energy adoption. Wildlife conservation efforts aim to reduce wildlife crimes by 30% and increase 
ecotourism revenue by 2%. 
Pollution control and monitoring will be strengthened through the establishment of 10 air quality 
monitoring stations to reduce air pollution levels by 20%. Additionally, an integrated waste 
management system in Mogadishu will target 50% waste recycling, a 35% reduction in pollutants, and 
the creation of 1,000 green jobs. These priorities provide a clear framework to advance Somalia’s green 
growth agenda, enhance climate resilience, and achieve sustainable development goals by 2029. 
Disaster Risk Reduction 
The Disaster Risk Reduction chapter prioritizes climate adaptation, improved disaster governance, 
and risk mitigation. Guided by national policies such as the National Climate Change Policy, National 
Environmental Policy, and National Adaptation Plan, Somalia’s approach aligns with global 
frameworks like the Paris Agreement and the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction. 
Through strategic investments and policy implementation, Somalia aims to enhance climate resilience, 
promote sustainable development, and safeguard natural resources for future generations. 
The focus is on strengthening resilience through proactive measures and reducing the number of 
people affected by disasters by 50% by 2029. Key priorities include preventive and mitigation 
strategies, such as transitioning to renewable energy sources like solar and wind power and 
modernizing water resource management with advanced technologies, including solar-powered 
systems and GIS mapping to address water scarcity and support climate adaptation.
