Central Bank of Somalia
It is my distinct honor to present the Fifth Issue of the Central Bank of Somalia 
(CBS) Statistical Bulletin. This publication is issued in accordance with the 
provisions of Articles 5(h), 44, and 55 of the Central Bank of Somalia Act, Law No. 
130 of April 2012. Under this Act, the CBS is mandated to serve as an economic 
advisor to the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS), to inform the public and 
relevant stakeholders of its policies, and to contribute to the development of sound 
economic policymaking for the country.
In fulfilling its responsibilities, the Central Bank of Somalia is committed to the 
regular dissemination of data through this Statistical Bulletin, ensuring accessibility 
for all relevant stakeholders. This edition presents data spanning the years 2022 
to 2025, covering key macroeconomic sectors: Monetary, Government Finance, 
Real, and External statistics. This Bulletin has been produced by the Statistics 
Division of the Research and Statistics Department under the Monetary, Financial, 
and Regulatory Policy Group of the Bank.
The Central Bank of Somalia would like to take this opportunity to thank FGS 
line ministries, departments, and agencies in providing the requisite data for the 
production of this Bulletin.
I hope all data stakeholders and end-users will find the content of this Bulletin 
useful and informative. CBS welcomes any feedback on this Bulletin or on any of 
its other publications.
Should you have any comments or reactions, please contact the Central Bank of 
Somalia.
Mr. Abdirahman Mohamed Abdullahi
Governor

Central Bank of Somalia
Introductory Notes
1.0 Analytical Balance Sheet of the 
Monetary Survey
The banking system, from which the data presented 
in this publication is obtained, consists of the 
Central Bank of Somalia (CBS), thirteen domestic 
commercial banks, and one foreign bank branch. 
It is important to note that financial institutions 
such as Mobile Money Operators, Insurance 
Companies, and Microfinance Institutions are not 
covered in the data for this edition, however, data 
for these financial institutions will be reported 
in subsequent releases.
Tables 1.1 and 1.2 present the monthly analytical 
balance sheets of the Central Bank of Somalia 
(CBS) and Other Depository Corporations 
(Commercial Banks).
The Depository Corporation Survey (DCS) data 
presented in Table 1.3 is derived by consolidating 
data from both the Central Bank (Table 1.1) and 
Commercial Banks (Table 1.2). These tables 
provide summary information that facilitates 
the preparation of comprehensive reports such 
as the DCS. The consolidation also supports 
effective cross-checking and validation of the 
summary reports.
2.0	Detailed Accounts of the Banking 
System
2.1	 Assets of Central Bank of Somalia
Table 2.1 presents the monthly Assets of the 
Central Bank of Somalia.
2.2	 Liabilities And Reserves of Central Bank Of 
Somalia
Table 2.2 presents the monthly Liabilities and 
Reserves of the Central Bank of Somalia.
2.3	 Consolidated Commercial Banks Balance 
Sheet
Table 2.3 encompasses aggregated monthly 
balance sheets of commercial banks. The 
aggregation/consolidation process involves 
summing the monthly balance sheet data of all 
commercial banks operating in Somalia.
2.4	 Consolidated Commercial Banks: Financing 
Assets 
Table 2.4 shows financing assets by instrument, 
derived from the consolidated data of commercial 
banks.
2.5	 Financial Soundness Indicators (FSIS) 
Table 2.5 presents percentages of the core 
Financial Soundness Indicators including Capital 
Adequacy, Asset Quality, Earnings & Profitability 
and Liquidity. It also presents additional FSIs 
and for Real Estate Markets (REM). 
2.6	 National Payments System (NPS) 
Table 2.6 depicts total National Payments System 
data derived from the RTGS & ACH by monthly 
volume and values. 
3.0	International Trade And Finance
3.1	 Workers’ Remittances
Table 3.1 presents Workers’ Remittances, which 
are current transfers sent for family maintenance 
by migrants who are employed and reside in 
other economies. A resident is defined as a 
person who stays, or is expected to stay, in an 
economy for one year or more excluding students, 
patients, military personnel, diplomats assigned 
to overseas embassies, and staff of international 
organizations.
3.2	 Somali Shilling Exchange Rates
Table 3.2 presents the Somali Shilling (SOS) 
exchange rates against the US Dollar (USD), 
showing three key indicators: the Buying Rate, 
the Selling Rate, and the Mid-Rate. The Buying 
Rate reflects the rate at which US Dollars are 
purchased using Somali Shillings, while the Selling 
Rate represents the rate at which US Dollars are 
sold in exchange for Somali Shillings. The Mid-
Rate is the average of the buying and selling 
rates and serves as a benchmark or indicative 
rate. These rates illustrate the behavior of the 
Somali Shilling in the foreign exchange market, 
indicating general trends in currency valuation, 
market stability, and monetary conditions over 
time.

3.3	 Balance Of Payments
The Balance of Payments Statement (Table 
3.3) is obtained from various sources including 
administrative data from the Central Bank of 
Somalia (CBS), Ministry of Finance (MoF) and 
the Somali National Bureau of Statistics (SNBS) 
and surveys done by the bank. The statement 
is in line with the International Monetary Fund’s 
Balance of Payments Manual, Sixth Edition 
(BPM6). The reserve assets section reports 
changes that have occurred in reserve assets 
within the reference period.
3.4	 Foreign Trade Summary
The foreign trade summary provides monthly 
total imports (CIF), total exports (FOB), and 
trade balance.
3.5	 Major Imported Commodities By Value
Table 3.5 presents the monthly value of major 
imported commodities, classified by product 
categories.
3.6	 Cements Imported Through Mogadishu 
Port
Table 3.6 presents the monthly volume and value 
of cement imported through Mogadishu Port.
3.7	 Imported Oil In Mogadishu Port
Table 3.7 presents data on the volume and value of 
oil imports through Mogadishu Port, categorized 
by type (Petrol, Jet A1, Diesel, LPG Gas).
3.8	 Major Exported Goods By Value And 
Volume
Table 3.8.1 presents monthly data on livestock 
exports, reported in terms of quantity (number 
of heads) and corresponding export value in 
U.S. Dollars.
Table 3.8.2 shows monthly export data for 
animal skin, and fresh meat, reported in terms 
of volume in pieces and tons respectively and 
corresponding export value in U.S. Dollars.
Table 3.8.3 presents the export of selected 
agricultural commodities namely sesame seed, 
sesame oil, and dry lemon reported in terms of 
volume and value in U.S. Dollars
Table 3.9 lists Somalia’s main trade partners by 
export destinations and import sources. It shows 
where Somalia sells its key products and where 
it imports most goods from, offering a snapshot 
of the country’s major trade relationships and 
its external economic engagement.
4.0	Public Finance 
4.1	 Government Revenue, Grants And 
Expenditure
Government revenue is classified as Tax or Non-
Tax. Tax Revenue comprises Import Duty, Excise 
Duty, Income Tax (including PAYE) and Value 
Added Tax (VAT). Non-Tax Revenue comprises 
Investment Income, Appropriations in Aid (AIA) 
and Other Levies relating to Traffic, Land, Airport, 
Licenses, Fines, and Forfeitures. Grants are Gift 
Funds received by the Government from other 
governments or international organizations. 
Government expenditure is classified into two, 
namely, Recurrent Expenditure and Development 
Expenditure (Capital Expenditure). Recurrent 
Expenditure refers to expenditures incurred 
persistently (e.g., monthly). It consists of Domestic 
and Foreign Payments, Wages and Salaries, 
Pensions and Payments for Maintenance and 
Operations. Development Expenditure refers to 
the expenditure incurred on projects including 
relevant salaries and wages.
5.0	General Economic Indicators
5.1	 Gross Domestic Product (Gdp): Current 
And Constant Prices
National Accounts Data are compiled by the 
Somali National Bureau of Statistics (SNBS). 
Annual GDP data are extracted from surveys 
conducted by SNBS and other official data 
producing agencies. The main data source for 
compiling the 2024 National Accounts was the 
2022 Somalia Integrated Household Budget 
Survey, and administrative data received from 
the institutions. 
5.2	 Consumer Price Index
 Data on Consumer Price Indices (CPIs) are 
collected by the SNBS measuring the weighted 
aggregate change in retail prices of goods and 
services purchased by households in Somalia. 
Based on the findings of the Somalia Integrated 
Household Budget Survey (SIHBS) conducted in 
2022, the CPI basket is classified into 13 categories

re-weighted to reflect the change in consumption

5.3	 Cereal Prices For Some Selected Regions
Tables 5.3.1 to 5.3.5 present monthly retail prices 
of selected cereal and staple food items across
