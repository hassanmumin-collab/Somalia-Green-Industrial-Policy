National Validation 
Workshop on the
Revision of the National Agricultural 
Mechanization Policy
The Department of Agricultural Technology and Innovation of the Ministry of Agricul-
ture and Irrigation (MoAI) organized a National Validation Workshop on the revision of 
the National Agricultural Mechanization Policy, which was held in Mogadishu from 7 
to 8 January 2026.  
The workshop marked a critical milestone in the national policy formulation process, 
providing a structured platform for stakeholders to review the revised policy, validate 
its strategic direction, and build consensus prior to finalization and formal approval.
=== PAGE 5 ===
Page 5
The workshop was officially opened by the 
Director General of the Ministry of Agricul-
ture and Irrigation, Eng. Mohamed Hassan 
Abdulle , who underscored the pivotal role 
of agricultural mechanization in accelerat-
ing Somalia’s agricultural transformation. 
He noted that reliance on traditional hand 
tools limits agricultural productivity, and 
emphasized the national objective to tran-
sition toward the use of modern agricultural 
machinery to improve efficiency and output. 
He highlighted mechanization as a key driver for increasing productivity, 
addressing labor constraints, strengthening climate resilience, and enhancing 
competitiveness across agricultural value chains.
The Director General further affirmed the Ministry’s commitment to lead the imple-
mentation of the National Agricultural Mechanization Policy, spearhead resource 
mobilization efforts, and work with government institutions and partners to support 
farmers’ access to agricultural machinery and equipment as a cornerstone of sus-
tainable agricultural development in Somalia.
The Director of the Department of Agricul-
tural Technology and Innovation, Eng. Dirie 
Abdi Mohamed, highlighted the importance 
of the National Agricultural Mechanization 
Policy and explained why Somalia requires 
a dedicated and comprehensive mechani-
zation policy at this stage of agricultural 
development. 
He emphasized that the revised policy is aligned with the National Transformation 
Plan (NTP) and is intended to address key structural constraints limiting agricul-
tural productivity, resilience, and competitiveness. He further emphasized that the 
revised policy aims to establish a coherent, enabling framework that supports pri-
vate sector–led, service-based, and sustainable mechanization development, while 
ensuring inclusivity for smallholder farmers and alignment with national develop-
ment priorities.
=== PAGE 6 ===
Page 6
The workshop brought together a broad and balanced group of participants, includ-
ing senior officials from the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation, officials 
from Federal Member State Ministries of Agriculture, private sector actors, machinery 
dealers, farmer cooperatives and producer representatives, academic and research 
institutions, and development partners. This inclusive participation ensured that per-
spectives from across the agricultural mechanization value chain were reflected in the 
validation process.
Participants engaged in structured group discussions and plenary sessions to review 
and validate the proposed policy interventions, strategic priorities, implementation 
modalities, and institutional arrangements. 
The discussions generated constructive, evidence-based feedback and practical rec-
ommendations aimed at strengthening the coherence, feasibility, and responsiveness 
of the policy to regional and sector-specific needs. Key comments and recommenda-
tions were consolidated and incorporated to further refine and improve the final draft 
of the National Agricultural Mechanization Policy.

During January 2026, the Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation implemented field 
activities in Afgooye District focusing on ecosystem restoration, integrated water 
resources management (IWRM), and strengthening community capacity in sustain-
able agriculture. the activities were coordinated and supervised by Eng. Abdirahim 
Adan Abdi, Project Coordinator, as part of the Ministry’s efforts to promote cli-
mate-resilient livelihoods and sustainable natural resource management.
Community awareness sessions were conducted on soil conservation, watershed 
protection, efficient use of water for agriculture, and the adoption of climate-smart 
farming practices. Under the guidance of Eng. Abdirahim Adan Abdi, farmers, water 
user groups, and local community members actively participated in the sessions, 
resulting in improved understanding of the relationship between environmental con-
servation, water availability, and agricultural productivity.
In addition, technical training and field-based support were provided to farmer groups 
through Farmer Field Schools and seed-saving initiatives. 
The training focused on improved agronomic practices, climate-smart agriculture, 
seed selection, and local seed preservation.These activities strengthened farmers’ 
practical skills, promoted peer-to-peer learning, and contributed to increased resil-
ience and reduced dependence on external seed supply.
Implementation during the reporting period faced challenges related to climate var-
iability, including irregular rainfall, drought conditions, limited access to agricultural 
inputs, and logistical constraints linked to infrastructure and security limitations.  To 
mitigate these challenges, the project promoted climate-smart approaches, flexi-
ble implementation aligned with seasonal forecasts, and strengthened coordination 
with local authorities and community structures. 
Overall, the January activities contributed positively to sustainable land and water 
management and improved the resilience of farming communities in Afgooye Dis-
trict.Planned actions for the following month include strengthening linkages between 
community-based and national early warning systems and continuing awareness 
initiatives on ecosystem restoration and integrated water resources management.

RESILIENCE STAKEHOLDERS’ 
MEETING ON RESPONDING
TO FOOD SECURITY CHALLENGES
Introduction
The Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation (MoAI) of the Federal Government of Somalia, 
through its Crop Production Department, participated in the Resilience Stakeholders’ 
Meeting on Responding to Food Security Challenges, a high-level consultative forum 
aimed at strengthening national coordination on food security and resilience-build-
ing interventions.
The meeting was convened and led by the Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation (MoAI–
FGS) in collaboration with the Ministry of Planning, the Ministry of Livestock, the Min-
istry of Fisheries, and international partners including FAO, alongside key national 
and international stakeholders working on resilience, climate adaptation, and food 
security.
The discussions focused on strengthening the resilience of vulnerable communities, 
enhancing agricultural production systems, and mitigating the impacts of climate 
change on food availability and access. Particular emphasis was placed on inte-
grated, multi-sectoral approaches and improved coordination among government 
institutions, development partners, and implementing stakeholders.
=== PAGE 12 ===
Page 12
In parallel with the stakeholders’ meeting, the Ministry officially launched its Call 
Center Office, marking the first fully operational agricultural call center established 
since the post-conflict period. Prior to this initiative, communication between the 
Ministry and farmers relied largely on informal and traditional methods, commonly 
known as “Taar” or “Hello Hello”, which limited structured information flow and ser-
vice efficiency.The establishment of the Call Center represents a major milestone in 
modernizing farmer–government engagement and strengthening agricultural ser-
vice delivery nationwide.

Strengthen coordination among govern-

Promote climate-resilient agricultural 
production systems to improve food 
availability, access, and stability for vul-
nerable communities.
Enhance communication mechanisms 
between the Ministry of Agriculture and 
Irrigation and farmers through the intro-

platform.
Improve farmers’ access to timely infor-
mation on agricultural services, techni-

Reinforce the Ministry’s commitment 
to implementing policies and programs 
aligned with national development

LAUNCH OF THE NATIONAL
AGRICULTURAL EXTENSION STRATEGY & 
ACTION PLAN (2026–2030)
The Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation (MoAI) marked a major milestone for Soma-
lia’s agricultural sector with the official launch of the National Agricultural Extension 
Strategy & Action Plan (2026–2030). 
The launch event, held on 19 January 2026 at the Villas Hotel in Mogadishu, brought 
together 50 key stakeholders from Federal Member States (FMS), development part-
ners, research and academic institutions, the private sector, farmer organizations, 
and extension service providers. total of 50 participants from states and other stake-
holders in agricultural sector.
=== PAGE 15 ===
Page 15
The January launch ceremony aimed to:
Officially introduce and socialize the National Agricultural Extension Strategy & 
Action Plan (2026–2030).
Present the Strategy’s vision, objectives, and strategic pillars to key stakeholders.
Foster dialogue between MoAI, Federal Member States, and agricultural partners 
on implementation modalities.
Collect initial stakeholder feedback to inform the rollout phase beginning in 2026.
Objectives of the Launch Event
           Event Facilitation and Methodology
The event was led by the Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation, with high-level leader-
ship provided by the State Minister of MoAI, who delivered the opening address and 
highlighted the Strategy’s relevance to national development and economic recovery.
Technical facilitation was provided by the Director of the Department of Research and 
Extension, who guided participants through the Strategy’s core components.
 The methodology combined:
	
Policy-level presentations setting the national context.
	
Visual and oral presentations detailing the Action Plan and its implementation
              framework.
	
Interactive question-and-answer sessions to promote clarity, ownership, 
              and stakeholder buy-in.
Participants engaged in discussions to identify immediate priorities and practical steps 
required to prepare for implementation at the start of 2026.
=== PAGE 16 ===
Page 16
Key Recommendations
Moving Forward
Stakeholders have recommended a 
series of actions to bolster the effective 
implementation of the initiative. These 
include mobilizing financial resources 
and cultivating technical partnerships in 
advance of the 2026 rollout, decentral-
izing the Strategy by educating regional 
and state-level extension officers on 
the updated guidelines, investing in 
digital extension platforms to broaden 
outreach and enhance operational effi-
ciency, incorporating gender-respon-
sive and youth-inclusive methodologies 
into all extension activities, enhanc-
ing coordination among public, private, 
and NGO extension service providers, 
and establishing a dedicated task force 
within the Department of Research and 
Extension to monitor progress against 
first-year milestones.
Conclusion
The January 2026 launch of the National 
Agricultural Extension Strategy & Action 
Plan marked a decisive step toward 
strengthening Somalia’s agricultural 
sovereignty and resilience. With broad 
stakeholder endorsement and strong 
leadership from the Ministry of Agri-
culture and Irrigation, the Strategy pro-
vides a clear and practical roadmap for 
improving farmers’ access to knowl-
edge, technologies, and innovation.
As preparations move forward, effective 
collaboration among Federal Member 
States, development partners, private 
sector actors, and farming communities 
will be essential. Successful implemen-
tation of the Strategy from 2026 to 2030 
is expected to contribute significantly 
to increased agricultural productivity, 
enhanced climate resilience, improved 
food security, and sustainable economic 
growth in Somalia.

The Desert Locust (DL) situation 
remained calm during January 2026. No 
surveys were conducted, however reports 
from district DL focal persons, scouts, 
and regional extension officers indicated 
that no locusts were seen in the coastal, 
sub-coastal, and inland areas of the 
locust breeding areas in the northwest 
and northeast regions Light to moderate 
rains that fell during the biggening of the 
month in the breeding areas may create 
favorable ecological conditions. vegeta-
tion is beginning to green, and the soil has 
become moist.
Forecasting
Ecological conditions in Somalia may 
become favorable for Desert Locust 
breeding in February 2026, particularly in 
northern coastal and sub-coastal areas 
such as Zaila, Lughaya, and Berbera dis-
tricts. Light to moderate rains, influenced 
by the Deyr rainy season, are expected 
in the northwest and northeast regions, 
increasing soil moisture and vegetation 
growth, which could create suitable con-
ditions for locust breeding.
