I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
A.  Background 
1.
This restructuring paper seeks Management approval for a Level 2 restructuring of the Somalia Urban Resilience 
Project – Phase II (SURP-II, P170922). The restructuring is proposed at the request of the Federal Republic of Somalia, as 
indicated in a Ministry of Finance letter dated March 5, 2026 (Ref: MOF/OM/0043/2026). The request comprises a US$17 
million partial cancellation from the project.  
Project Status
2.
The SURP-II, effective since February 2020, aims to strengthen local government service delivery capacity and 
expand access to climate-resilient urban infrastructure. The project builds on nearly a decade of urban engagement in 
Somalia, beginning in 2016 with the Somalia Urban Investment Planning Project (P150374) and followed by the SURP, 
Phase I (P163857).  The current project closing date is December 2028.
3.
Implementation status. As of March 2026, cumulative disbursements amount to US$135 million, representing 61 
percent of the total US$278.5 million grant. Progress toward achievement of the Project Development Objective and 
Overall Implementation Progress are both rated Satisfactory, with most ratings remaining above Moderately Satisfactory 
1over the past 12 months. Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) performance is rated Moderately Unsatisfactory; 
however, this has not affected overall implementation progress, and corrective actions are being addressed within the 
existing project management and oversight arrangements. Despite operating in a complex environment, the project is 
being successfully implemented across seven strategic cities. Implementation arrangements comprise seven municipal 
Project Implementation Units (PIUs), supported by a federal Project Coordination Unit (PCU) that provides technical 
backstopping, coordination, and quality assurance, as well as inter-ministerial project steering committees at both the 
state and federal levels that offer strategic guidance and oversight. The project remains fully compliant with all legal 
covenants, including those related to financial management and audits.
4.
Achievements to date. To date, nearly 700,000 people have benefited from improved urban infrastructure, of 
whom 51 percent of infrastructure beneficiaries are women, over 1 million received life-saving drought assistance, and 
373,702 person-days of employment have been generated for 13,000 local workers. Civil works in most project cities are 
expected to conclude by December 2025, with Hargeisa completed by the current project closing date.2  The United 
Nations Office for Project Services is providing design and supervision across the six cities. Drought response activities, 
including cash transfer, water and sanitation facilities, and temporary housing, land, and property have been successfully 
completed in September 2024, providing life-saving assistance to more than 1 million people. SURP-II has already helped 
better define intergovernmental functional roles and responsibilities related to urban services. With relatively strong 
implementation arrangements and broad geographic coverage, SURP-II has further evolved to support cities on resilient

three in Beledweyne, and two in Dhusamareeb, with a total value of US$64.6 million and expected completion by September 2026. Preparation of major drainage

ready to invite bids for a US$17 million trunk line, with an additional US$18.5 million expected under the MPA. Feasibility studies and detailed designs are ongoing in

reference have been prepared for the design of 45 km of roads and one bridge, with feasibility studies underway to confirm priority investments and inform 
engineering designs for the US$20 million envelope by October 2025.

B. Rationale
5.
The Restructuring Paper proposes the cancellation of US$17 million from the International Development 
Association grant under Component 1 of the project, which was originally allocated to drainage works under the Banadir 
Regional Administration (BRA). At the Government’s request, received on March 5, 2026, these funds may be reallocated 
to the proposed Somalia Transformational Access and Infrastructure Resilience for Sustainable Jobs Program (STAIR2JOBS, 
P513127). This reallocation would enable the pooling of resources with other available financing to support larger-scale 
drainage investments in Mogadishu and a longer implementation timeline, thereby facilitating timely and effective 
completion. As a result, the project’s total budget envelope will be reduced from US$278.5 million to US$261.5 million. 
The proposed cancellation also requires the reallocation of  US$3.7 million in uncommitted funds from  Components 1 
and 2 to Component 3 (Project Management) to address capacity gaps and support the scaled STAIR2JOBS investment 
portfolio, by cost-sharing PIU functions for the STAIRS2JOBS delivery at PCU and Banadir PIU level,  along with 
corresponding adjustments to the project’s results framework. 3 The restructuring does not trigger any policy or waivers. 
The cancellation does not have any implications for the Environmental and Social Safeguards under the project.
6.
The proposed restructuring is justified by the technical complexity, scale, and sequencing requirements of the 
Mogadishu trunk drainage system, which is designed as a large, integrated network to manage a 1-in-25-year flood 
event under climate-informed scenarios. Detailed feasibility studies, engineering designs, and ESF requirements have 
already been completed under SURP-II, providing a robust technical and safeguards foundation for scaled implementation. 
The drainage solution involves an extensive main trunk and outlet system, expanded conveyance capacity, multiple flood 
control structures, and nature-based solutions, all of which must function as a coherent system and be constructed over 
a longer timeline to ensure hydraulic integrity and resilience under normal, high-tide, and worst-case sea-level conditions. 
Fragmenting these investments under the current project constrains packaging and limits market interest, whereas 
consolidating financing under a larger, longer-horizon program enables procurement of appropriately sized works that 
better reflect the technical and risk profile of the infrastructure. This approach is expected to attract more qualified and 
experienced bidders capable of delivering complex urban drainage systems at scale, improve competition and value for 
money, and reduce implementation and performance risks while laying the foundation for future phases, including 
real-time flood warning systems.
