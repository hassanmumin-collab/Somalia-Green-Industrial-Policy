Quantify the service delivered by the incumbent technology
a.
E.g. how much heating in buildings, vehicle miles traveled, metric tons of steel, electricity 
generated…
4.
Compute the CO2 emissions for the climate solution to deliver the same 
service in the same market area.

=== PAGE 7 ===
1a, Technical potential: Example questions
These are pure energy accounting questions. Examples:
●
Demand side: How much energy does the carbon emitting 
technology consume? How much zero carbon energy do we need 
for its substitute?
●
Supply side: How big is the zero carbon energy resource, how 
does it compare to what we need?
How big are the efficiency penalties? Can they be overcome? 
Examples:
●
Solar needs (inefficient) energy storage; may need to be curtailed
●
Rankine cycle in a nuclear generator is only ~40% efficient.
=== PAGE 8 ===
Step 1b: Implied cost per ton
The challenge? Determine what units to use for costs and emissions
Option 1: Costs and emissions for total market area
Option 2: Costs and emissions per unit of stuff
"Stuff" could be kWh, buildings, pounds of steel, airline flight miles, etc.
Cost per 
metric ton
=
Climate solution cost - status quo cost
Status quo CO2 - Climate solution CO2

=== PAGE 11 ===
Step 2b: Who pays and how?
●
When we have an implied cost of carbon, how will society pay for it, and how 
will that impact stakeholders?
Two ways to think about this:
●
Private costs to the consumers and businesses that purchase and use the 
technology solution. 
●
Total social cost to deploy the technology solution. 
Why would social cost and private costs differ?
●
Governments could spend money to support solutions (we'll talk about that in 
this class)
●
Societal externalities (we won't talk about that in this class)

=== PAGE 13 ===
Duncan's summary: ways to offset climate solutions costs
Reduce capex
Investment Tax Credits (ITCs), Grants, Accelerated 
Depreciation.
Reduce opex
Production Tax Credits (PTCs), Feed-in Tariffs (FITs), 
Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs).
Price carbon
Carbon Taxes, Emissions Trading Schemes (Cap-and-Trade).
Standards
Renewable Portfolio Standards (RPS), EV Sales Mandates, 
Energy Efficiency Standards.
Subsidize R&D
Government-funded National Labs, University Grants, 
Innovation Prizes.
De-risking
Loan Guarantees, Direct Green Public Infrastructure 
Spending, Advance Market Commitments (AMCs).
=== PAGE 14 ===
Step 3: In sum, evaluate potential for speed and scale
1.
What are the warts to a solution…what needs to improve?
2.
What is the size of the total addressable market? (How much carbon can the 
solution save?)
3.
Does the solution have “voltage,” i.e. the power to beat the status quo? 
Beware of confirmation bias as you evaluate the data to answer this question. 
(Cribbed from John List's Speed and Scale book)
4.
Are people really willing to pay? Be skeptical. What are the realistic 
opportunities to reduce private costs?
